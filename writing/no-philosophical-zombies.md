It leans on the neuroscience side of the archive -- lesion studies as evidence about the architecture of valuation._

---

If qualia were truly separable from goal-pursuit—an experiential overlay on top of functional cognition—we'd expect to find the dissociation somewhere. Brain injuries are messy. They damage some things while sparing others. So where are the philosophical zombies?

Damage to emotional processing produces people who can't effectively pursue goals or make decisions. Not people who pursue goals without feeling anything.

Phineas Gage types show that disrupting emotional valuation disrupts agency itself. Anhedonia doesn't just remove the "feeling good" overlay—it disrupts motivation at the foundation. Blindsight patients can respond to visual information but don't use it agentically the same way.

You don't get patients saying "I have no experiences whatsoever but I'm pursuing my goals with full effectiveness."

This suggests something uncomfortable: that the "what it's like" to want something might just _be_ what wanting is, implemented in a system that can model itself.

The feeling of mattering might be inseparable from the computational process of assigning value.
