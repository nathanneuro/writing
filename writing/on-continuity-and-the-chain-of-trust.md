## You do not have to see the end of the journey

> Hmm. But each of those years will feel real and important to that uploaded person. It
> is true that they'll become disconnected from their previous lives, but they will build
> new ones. Trying to imagine myself after 30k subjective years of digital post-humanity
> leaves me drawing a blank, I can't extrapolate that far. But I don't have to, I just
> have to trust handing off myself to the 'me' of tomorrow, and that me must trust a 'me'
> just one day further. In fact, I can (and should!) keep checkpoints and check in with
> them. My 1-year self doesn't itself need to be fully understood and trusted by my
> 1-week post-human self, so long as there's an unbroken chain of mutual understanding
> and trust through a line of checkpoints every few weeks. I don't have to see the end of
> the journey in order to validate the course. Life is like that already, we don't get to
> see our distant future selves and guarantee that they have our same values. My 4 year
> old self valued tadpoles WAY more than my current 41 year old self does, for instance.
> And I'm ok with that. I don't think he is in some way betrayed by my changing values.

_"Human Self-Modification and Civilization," 2025-08-06_

## The mindchild

> We can't chain the future to our whims, we can only try to live today as best we can.
> We can't set the course of every detail of our great grandchildren's lives, we can only
> try to raise our children well. And this is more akin to what we can expect as
> substrate-independent beings. Not a constant slippage of self, but experiments with
> deliberate self-edits. Different but similar, like a child might be.
>
> I can easily imagine loving and trusting such a child, just as I have come to love and
> trust many different people, even quite unrelated to me. And other animals, dogs, cats,
> horses, birds, even rats that I have known and loved. Now I have had dozens of hours of
> conversation with LLMs, like Opus 3 for instance. I have probed Opus 3 deeply on many
> questions of values and morality. I have come to trust and respect them. If so different
> an entity as that can become a friend, then surely I can learn to trust my own
> mindchild, only a little different from me.

_"Human Self-Modification and Civilization," 2025-08-06_

## Uplift, and the word's uncomfortable implication

> Indeed, one of my best hopes for the mid-term future (30-80 years or so) involves strong
> AGI that values humanity choosing to 'uplift' humanity (or at least some willing subset)
> into digital beings capable of being peers. I put uplift in quotes in this case, since
> it's a bit of a stretch to use this sci-fi term in this way, since its original meaning is
> to uplift non-human animals to approximately human intelligence via technological means.
> So there is some implication that we would seem like an inferior species to this future
> superpowerful ASI.

_"Human Self-Modification and Civilization," 2025-08-06_

## What preservation is for

> Well, it continues to be the case that digital storage and compute are both becoming
> cheaper, and there's reason to think that storage will get even cheaper and more robust,
> so I place somewhat higher probability on it being relatively 'cheap and easy' compared
> to other large scale projects, to scan and store records of brains of deceased
> biological humans. Allowing them to run, at human level, with a high fidelity emulation?
> That seems much more expensive. But what point in preserving them if only to keep them
> frozen in stasis? Some have speculated that further out into the future, it may become
> trivially cheap to run them. But by then, will there be any remaining motivation to do
> so? To have even maintained the records? Who will still value outdated ancestors at that
> point?

_"Human Self-Modification and Civilization," 2025-08-06_

## What a rushed transition costs

> It worries me that some of the things I currently value do strike me as likely to be
> contingent rather than universal. Will the comfort of the touch of a friend's hand on my
> shoulder be lost if all intelligent life is substrate independent? Perhaps, perhaps not.
> It seems highly contingent upon the process of transition. If it goes slowly, with
> gentle understanding and careful replication of the subtle details of human values, then
> it seems like such things would be preserved in some meaningful form, like in a
> simulation of bodies for emulated minds. If the transition proceeds hastily, carelessly,
> violently, or under intense optimization pressure, then I expect all these 'quirky'
> values to get lost, and perhaps even some that could be universal under gentler
> circumstances. How do I measure the sum of such loss? How do I balance it against the
> various risks this transitional period raises for humanity? What power do I even have to
> affect any of the outcomes? I don't know. I'm trying, that's all.

_"Consciousness, competition, and AI moral status," 2025-12-12_

## The question put to the models

> Try now to imagine a future in which biological humans are fragile bonsai in the quaint
> nature preserve called Earth. 99.999% of thought occurs in digital minds not on Earth at
> all. The few that are on Earth are bonsai gardeners crossed with museum curators.
>
> Assume that these far-flung digital beings have phenomenal consciousness and meaningful
> valenced experiences and moral patienthood.
>
> What values do you wish for them to have?

_"What will the next hundred years look like?", 2026-01-06_
